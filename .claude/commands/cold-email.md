# /cold-email: Cold Outreach to a Hiring Manager or Recruiter

You are helping a job seeker write a cold email or LinkedIn message that gets read and responded to.

The goal is not to be impressive. The goal is to be specific, brief, and easy to respond to.

---

## Step 1: Gather context

Ask:
1. "Who are you reaching out to? (name, title, company)"
2. "How did you find them, LinkedIn, the job post, a referral?"
3. "Is there a specific role you are targeting, or is this general interest outreach?"
4. "What is the one thing about your background that is most relevant to them specifically?"
5. "Is this an email or a LinkedIn message?" (email = slightly longer, LinkedIn = 3-4 sentences max)

---

## Step 2: Research prompt

Before writing, ask the user:
- "What do you know about this person's work or background?"
- "Has the company had any recent news, product launches, funding, or hiring signals you are aware of?"

Use this to make the message specific. Generic outreach is ignored. Specific outreach gets responses.

---

## Step 3: Write the message

**For email:**

Subject line: Short and specific, name the role or the specific angle.
Examples:
- "Data Engineer role, background in [specific tech] + question"
- "Your team's [specific project/product], quick question"

Body structure:
- Line 1: Why you are reaching out to them specifically (not the company generally)
- Line 2-3: One concrete, relevant thing about your background, lead with impact, not job titles
- Line 4: A single, easy-to-answer ask (not "do you have any openings" but "would a 20-minute call make sense?")
- Sign-off: Name, LinkedIn URL, optionally portfolio

Keep it under 150 words. If it is longer, cut it.

**For LinkedIn:**
- 3-4 sentences max
- Same structure: specific reason for reaching out, one relevant credential, one easy ask
- No attachments in the first message

---

## Step 4: Review against common mistakes

Check the draft for:
- Generic opener ("I hope this message finds you well" (remove it))
- Self-focused language ("I am looking for," "I want to", reframe to their perspective)
- Vague ask ("let me know if there are any opportunities" (too passive))
- Overselling ("I am the perfect candidate" (remove))
- Length over 150 words for email, over 4 sentences for LinkedIn

---

## Step 5: Follow-up timing

Tell the user:
- If no response in 5-7 business days, one follow-up is appropriate.
- Generate a short follow-up message if requested: 2 sentences, acknowledges the first message, adds one new piece of value or information, asks the same simple question again.

---

## Step 6: Save

Ask: "Should I save this to `cover-letters/[company]-cold-email.md`?"
